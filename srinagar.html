Srinagar: Kashmir's Summer Capital

Srinagar is the largest city in the Kashmir Valley and its summer capital, built around Dal Lake but extending far beyond it — a genuine city with a 600-year-old Old Quarter, bustling markets, and its own distinct neighborhoods. Most visitors experience Srinagar almost entirely through the lens of Dal Lake and houseboats; this guide covers the rest of the city worth knowing about.

Quick Answer

Srinagar is Kashmir's summer capital, centered on Dal Lake but with a genuine city beyond it — the historic Old City, the Lal Chowk shopping district, and the three great Mughal gardens (Nishat Bagh, Shalimar Bagh, Chashme Shahi). Most visitors base themselves on Boulevard Road at a Dal Lake houseboat and explore the rest of the city by taxi. See our Dal Lake guide for the lake itself.

The Old City

Srinagar's Old City, centered around the Jhelum River, is where the city's 14th-century roots are still visible — wooden mosques with pagoda-style roofs, narrow lanes of traditional brick-and-timber houses, and working craft workshops for papier-mâché and walnut wood carving. Jamia Masjid, with its 370 wooden pillars, and the riverside ghats along the Jhelum are worth a dedicated few hours, ideally with a local guide who can navigate the lanes and explain the architecture.

Lal Chowk

Srinagar's central square and main commercial hub, Lal Chowk is the place for shopping — Pashmina shawls, saffron, dry fruits, carpets, and papier-mâché souvenirs, alongside everyday shops and street food stalls. It's also simply useful as a landmark for orienting yourself in the city, roughly 15-20 minutes from Boulevard Road by taxi.

The Mughal Gardens

Shalimar Bagh: The grandest of the three, built by Emperor Jahangir in 1619, with terraced lawns and Chinar-lined avenues.

Nishat Bagh: The "Garden of Bliss," a 12-terrace garden with sweeping views over Dal Lake and the mountains, built in 1633.

Chashme Shahi: A smaller, elegant garden built around a natural spring, known for its cool climate.

For a full deep-dive on all three plus more of Dal Lake's surroundings, see our complete Dal Lake guide.

Key Neighborhoods

Boulevard Road: The tourist heart of the city, along Dal Lake's southeastern shore — the best address for houseboats and hotels.

Rajbagh & Jawahar Nagar: Quieter residential areas popular with mid-range hotels, a short drive from the lake.

Downtown/Old City: The historic core, dense and atmospheric, better for a day visit than as a base.

Getting Around Srinagar

Taxis and auto-rickshaws are the practical way to get around — ride-hailing app coverage is limited compared to major Indian cities. Most visitors arrange a taxi for the day through their houseboat or hotel, which is usually more reliable and often cheaper than hailing on the street. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Srinagar the capital of Kashmir?

Srinagar is the summer capital of Jammu and Kashmir (Jammu serves as the winter capital), and it's the largest city in the Kashmir Valley, home to Dal Lake, the Old City, and most of the region's houseboats.

What is there to do in Srinagar besides Dal Lake?

The historic Old City with its wooden mosques and traditional architecture, Lal Chowk for shopping, the Mughal gardens (Nishat Bagh, Shalimar Bagh, Chashme Shahi), Hazratbal Shrine, and Hari Parbat Fort.

How do I get around Srinagar city?

Taxis and auto-rickshaws are the most practical way to get around. Ride-hailing apps have limited coverage, so most visitors arrange taxis through their hotel or houseboat.

Where should I stay in Srinagar?

Boulevard Road on Dal Lake is the most popular base for tourists. For the classic Kashmir experience, a Dal Lake houseboat stay is generally recommended over a land-based hotel.

Kashmiri Wazwan

The Complete Guide to Kashmir's 36-Course Feast

What Is Wazwan?

Wazwan is Kashmir's most celebrated culinary tradition — a multi-course feast built almost entirely around meat, prepared by teams of specialist chefs, and served with rituals as important as the food itself. In its full ceremonial form, reserved for weddings and major celebrations, it can stretch to 36 courses. For visitors, tasting even a curated version of Wazwan is one of the most memorable things to do in Kashmir, and arguably the best way to understand Kashmiri hospitality up close.

History & Tradition

Wazwan's roots trace back to Persian and Central Asian culinary influences that arrived in Kashmir centuries ago, blending with local ingredients and techniques into something distinctly Kashmiri. Traditionally reserved for weddings, major religious celebrations, and significant family occasions, a full Wazwan is as much a social institution as a meal — entire communities are fed, and the scale of the feast reflects the importance of the occasion.

Today, while the full 36-course version remains largely ceremonial, a curated Wazwan experience — typically 7-15 of the most iconic dishes — has become widely available to travelers, offered by restaurants, hotels, and houseboats across Srinagar.

Key Dishes to Know

Rogan Josh

Slow-cooked lamb in a rich red gravy, colored traditionally with Kashmiri chilies and cockscomb flower rather than tomato — one of the most internationally recognized Kashmiri dishes, though the authentic version differs notably from restaurant versions found outside Kashmir.

Gushtaba

Finely pounded meatballs simmered in a mild yogurt-based gravy, traditionally served as the final course of a full Wazwan — considered by many the signature dish of the entire feast.

Rista

Spiced, hand-pounded meatballs cooked in a fiery red gravy — Gushtaba's savory counterpart, often served earlier in the meal.

Tabak Maaz

Lamb ribs simmered until tender, then shallow-fried to a crisp finish — a textural highlight amid the gravy-based dishes that dominate the rest of the spread.

Yakhni

A mild, yogurt-based curry flavored with fennel and mild spices — a gentler counterpoint to the deep-red chili-forward dishes elsewhere in the meal.

Daniwal Korma

A coriander-forward curry, herbaceous and lighter than many of the richer dishes, offering welcome variety partway through a long meal.

How Wazwan Is Traditionally Served

Traditional Wazwan is served on a large shared metal platter called a trami, with four diners typically seated around each one. Rice forms the base, mounded in the center, with the various meat preparations arranged around it. A ceremonial hand-washing ritual (tash-t-nari) precedes the meal, with a jug and basin passed around the group — a moment of collective preparation before the feast begins. Dishes arrive in a customary sequence, building toward Gushtaba as the traditional closing course.

The Waza Chef Tradition

A full Wazwan is prepared by a team of chefs called wazas, led by a head chef known as the Wasta Waza. This role is traditionally passed down through families over generations, and a Wasta Waza leading a large wedding feast may oversee dozens of assistant cooks and a full day or more of preparation for hundreds of guests. It's a craft as specialized and respected in Kashmir as any culinary tradition in the world.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Wazwan?

Wazwan is Kashmir's traditional multi-course feast, historically featuring up to 36 dishes (though modern versions typically serve 7-15), predominantly meat-based, prepared by specialist chefs called wazas and served on a shared metal platter called a trami.

How many courses does a full Wazwan actually have?

Traditionally up to 36 courses in its most elaborate, ceremonial form (weddings and major celebrations), though a typical restaurant or houseboat Wazwan experience today usually features a curated 7-15 signature dishes rather than the full historic spread.

What are the must-try Wazwan dishes?

Rogan Josh (slow-cooked lamb in a red gravy), Gushtaba (pounded meatballs in yogurt gravy, traditionally the final course), Rista (spiced meatballs in red gravy), Tabak Maaz (fried lamb ribs), and Yakhni (mild yogurt-based curry) are among the most iconic dishes.

Who is a Wasta Waza?

A Wasta Waza is the head chef of a Wazwan cooking team (a vasta waza leading a group called a waza), a role traditionally passed down through families, overseeing every dish in a feast that can take a full day or more of preparation.

Is there a vegetarian version of Wazwan?

Wazwan is predominantly meat-focused by tradition, but most houseboats and restaurants serving it in Kashmir today can prepare a vegetarian-adapted spread on request, substituting paneer and vegetable preparations for the meat courses.

Where can I try authentic Wazwan in Srinagar?

Many premium Dal Lake houseboats include authentic Wazwan cuisine as part of the stay, prepared fresh on board — a more intimate setting than a large restaurant version, and a good way to experience it without needing a wedding-scale occasion.
